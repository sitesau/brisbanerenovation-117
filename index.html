Renovation Builders Brisbane Cost Guide Explained

When comparing renovation builders in Brisbane, the initial step is not about aesthetics but about establishing a clear brief, a realistic budget, and understanding the scope of work. Renovation paths vary widely, encompassing kitchens, bathrooms, Queenslander renovations, house extensions, full home makeovers, and heritage projects. Each of these demands different considerations before a price can be determined.

A kitchen renovation brief might focus on layout, finishes, and cabinetry. A bathroom brief requires decisions on fixtures and spatial arrangements. A full home renovation involves a complex sequence of structural work, flooring, electrical, plumbing, painting, and fixture installation. It is crucial to use these categories as a scoping tool, not merely a checklist.

Before requesting quotes, homeowners should document the affected rooms, what will be retained, what will change, and whether the project alters the building's footprint or structure. Brisbane's diverse housing stock, from classic Queenslanders to modern designs, means that site conditions can significantly impact project complexity and cost, even for projects with similar budget labels.

Cost Signals to Check Before Getting a Quote

Understanding indicative cost ranges can help homeowners gauge the feasibility of their renovation plans before investing significant time in detailed pricing. The following figures provide useful starting points:

  • Kitchens typically range from $25,000 to $60,000+.
  • Bathrooms generally fall between $22,000 to $35,000+.
  • House extensions often start from $150,000.
  • Full home renovations typically begin from $150,000+.
  • A standard 3-4 bedroom home renovation can range from $250,000 to $450,000.
  • Raise and build-under projects commence from $650,000.

These figures are not a substitute for a measured quote but help test whether a proposed brief aligns with the expected cost band. A quote request should clearly state the rooms involved, the preferred finish level, whether layout changes are anticipated, and any known structural work. Resources like ASIC Moneysmart's guidance on renovating are valuable for treating renovation spending as a budget decision, not just a design one.

Approvals, Timing, and Documentation Requirements

Council approval is generally required when renovations alter a building's footprint or structure. A building permit is necessary for structural alterations, extensions, and significant layout changes. Internal-only renovations typically do not require planning approval, but properties in a heritage overlay will need additional consent.

Timing should be discussed early in the process. Council approval can take between 2 to 8 weeks, depending on complexity. Broad renovation timelines include:

  • Full home renovations: typically 5-12 months (including approvals).
  • Kitchen renovations: 4-8 weeks.
  • Bathroom renovations: 2-4 weeks.

Key questions to ask include whether the project changes the structure, footprint, or layout, and if a certifier, town planner, or specific council pathway is needed. Understanding how approval timing affects ordering, demolition, and the start date is crucial for accurate project management.

Builder Checks and Quote Comparison

When selecting renovation builders in Brisbane, verifying credentials and ensuring quotes are directly comparable is paramount. Builders in Queensland must hold a current QBCC licence for work over $3,300. Membership in industry bodies like the Housing Industry Association (HIA) and Master Builders Australia indicates professional standards.

Homeowners should request detailed quotes that clearly separate the scope of work, exclusions, and assumptions. A good quote comparison involves ensuring each builder has priced the same rooms, the same level of finish, the same approval pathway, and the same site assumptions. A quote that omits essential elements like approvals or structural uncertainty is not directly comparable to one that includes them.

Define the scope precisely by listing rooms, structural changes, preferred finishes, and any heritage or Queenslander features before requesting quotes. Check the approval path by asking about structural, layout, or footprint changes and the need for professional review. Request comparable quotes by giving each builder the same brief.

  1. Define Your Project Scope. List all rooms affected, specify desired finishes, and note any structural changes or heritage features before requesting quotes.
  2. Understand Approval Requirements. Determine if council approval or a building permit is needed based on structural, layout, or footprint changes. Consult a certifier or town planner if necessary.
  3. Request Comparable Quotes. Provide each builder with the same detailed brief to ensure quotes cover identical scope, finishes, and approval pathways.
  4. Verify Builder Credentials. Check for current QBCC licences and inquire about their experience with similar projects. Membership in industry bodies is also a good indicator.
  5. Factor in Timing and Sequencing. Discuss project timelines, including council approval duration, material lead times, and construction schedules, with your builder.
